Celerity BBS was a popular platform for the "elite" BBS online community in the early 1990s that heavily focused on colorful text artwork, message boards, and file sharing. The software is credited to Mobius, and [The Byter](https://demozoo.org/sceners/46496/), sysop of Lexicon of the Cabal and Lexicon CAE, who ran the DOS PC software on his own systems. But it has been [speculated](http://software.bbsdocumentary.com/IBM/DOS/FORUM/) the software is originally based off the source code from a late 1980s application named Forum-PC v2.5 by Ken Duda. And, the later revisions of Celerity had plenty of tools, utilities, code, and artwork supplied by various users and fans of the platform.

The release 1.20 documentation, "Celerity is a Forum hack which attempts to take the best features of all the other boards and combine them into one system. It is programmed by The Byter and Mobius, and is a presentation of [The Alternative](/g/the_alternative). It draws heavily on [TCS](https://demozoo.org/groups/159601/) and KauCom (a board for the Apple II which I wrote a number of years ago), and I have also picked up a couple ideas from the other Forum hacks out there."

While originally invitation only software, later releases offered donation access, only to pivot into fully paid software distributed in a shareware model.

June 1990, _Celerity is NOT a public access bulletin board system. I do not intend to give it a wide release like TCS and Emulex have, nor do I intend to keep it as isolated and proprietary as some others are. At the moment, I will only allow a certain number of systems to run it as beta sites. I plan on keeping this number at about ten systems, and no more._

September 1990, _Every man has his price. If your system does not meet the above qualifications, but you are still very interested in running Celerity, we have been known to
allow systems full Celerity access and rights in exchange for a donation. These donations range between \$70 and \$150_

October 1990, _To be invited to run the software, your BBS must come to the attention of the authors (The Byter or Mobius), and meet the following requirements: 2: Gets new software within 0-1 days of its release. 5: Is affiliated with a respectable pirate group. 6: Is in an area where there are no Celerity systems. 7: The board must be established._

December 1990, _Celerity is now being distributed solely on a shareware basis, which entails a $50 registration fee._ and _Celerity BBS may be obtained on many bulletin boards throughout the country, but only as a demo version. If you wish to run a system with more than ten users and use your own handle as sysop, then you must get a registered copy. To acquire a registered copy, you must send in the shareware fees in the form of a check or money order (payable to The Lexicon)_

The platform also operated an exclusive message board network known as CelerityNet where users of each Celerity BBS could send private messages to the networked boards, and post in public "conferences" which were message spaces based on a subject or theme. Often with a focus on "elite" art, piracy, and nerd computing. The software document mentioned the following conferences for November 1990.
