**Forum-PC** is a granddaddy of PC BBS software and was very influential for the "elite" online communities of that early BBS era. Created by Kenneth J. Duda (aka Ken Duda) while in high school, Forum was likely a fun hobby project for Ken more than a commercial enterprise. At the time Ken was living in Menlo Park, next door to Palo Alto in Silicon Valley, so he was well in proximity to the booming micro and computer industry as it was happening. And Ken's choice of software name could have been inspired by one of the first popular BBS platforms, "Forum 80", first [released](<https://www.trs-80.com/images/covers/magcover-PROG-80-Magazine-Vol-1-No-07-(1980)(SoftSide-Publications)[80-04][OCR].jpg>) for the Tandy TRS-80, in 1980.

The earliest surviving code of Forum is Forum-PC 2.10 with a &copy; 1986 copyright. Unfortunately, the copy is a ZIP file repack from 1990 and the source files have 1988 metadata. The original 1986 package would have been distributed in an earlier file format such as ARC by SEA.

Ken charged $50 for Forum-PC and provided the source code to registered users but also support through his personal BBS. The "Forum-PC Demo BBS" that operated as central online hub for users of the software, to get updates, chat to the author, report bugs, and discover fellow users. However, it is uncertain if any binaries of Form-PC were provided, or if users had to build the software themselves using [Borland Turbo Pascal](https://winworldpc.com/product/turbo-pascal/3x). Ken was pretty clear on the use case of the source code, _You may also make any modifications you wish. However, you may NOT distribute this program, unmodified or modified, to people who are not registered users._

In 1987, possibly mid-year in summer, Ken released Forum-PC 3.0 which completely dropped the $50 registration expectation and instead released it as conditional freeware. _This program, and all of its utilities, and all source code, is in the public domain. You are free to modify it in any way, and to distribute those modifications._ But confusingly, "in the public domain" has a very different meaning to public domain software, which some people later mixed up. _This program and its source code are in the public domain. You may distribute it to whomever you please provided that you accept no remuneration_.

It is not certain why Ken dropped the fee, maybe he knew he couldn't continue working on it once he went to university? Or maybe he discovered boards online running unregistered copies and preferred developing a community over demanding financial compensation? _If you modify Forum-PC in a useful way, or write Forum-PC utilities, please call and upload them! I hope to collect (and write) a lot of useful programs and enhancements for Forum-PC on my BBS._ And _I'd love to hear from you! Please send me any comments you have about Forum-PC. And, like most public domain contributors, the thought of donations lurks in my subconsious (especially as I'm headed towards college next year...)!_

_Hello! Welcome to Forum-PC version 3.00. Forum-PC has been completely re-written for this version. There are doubtlessly many bugs at this point. Please don't try to take this system seriously_. Maybe because of this, it wasn't the version 3 code that got repurposed by the "elite" Scene. Instead it was Ken's earlier release of Forum-PC v2.5 that was reused, which is different in design and execution. Unfortunately it is code that is lost, at least in the original unmodified form from 1986 or 1987.

It's hard to overstate the impact of Forum-PC on the online underground in the mid-1980s to the 1990s. Ken Sallot, author of the LSD BBS platform would later [state](http://software.bbsdocumentary.com/IBM/DOS/FORUM/) that Emulex, TCS, Celerity, USSR, Vision, Oblivion, Monarch, and of course his own software LSD, as well as products Sallot didn't mention. They were all derived directly or indirectly from the Duda's code. Possibly hundreds of "elite" hacking, phreak, wares, and art boards were created using these platforms.

Thanks to a rare and early "elite" [PC BBS list](/f/b8246fd) created in February 1987 by software programmer and cracker Sam Brown for his Byte Bandits BBS. We know he thought there were over 25 worthwhile boards running either Forum-PC, or his derivative Emulex.

In late 1989, former hacker Shooting Shark would [interview](https://archive.org/details/microtimeissue5100unse/mode/2up?q=%22forum+pc+software%22) in Phrack issue 33 talking about the mid-1980s. _I called The Matrix, run by Dr. Strangelove. This was my first experience with Ken’s FORUM-PC BBS software._ Later continuing with _Shortly after that, The Matrix went down and was quickly replaced by IDI, run by Aiken Drum. It is here that I met Sir Francis Drake. Shortly after THAT, IDI went down and was quickly replaced by Lunatic Labs Unltd, run by my old friend The Mad Alchemist. TMA lived within walking distance of my house, so I called LunaLabs quite a bit. LunaLabs later became the home base of Phrack for a few issues when Knight Lightning and Taran King gave it upon entering their freshman year of college._ And confirming, _The Matrix, IDI, Lunatic Labs -- Three great Bay Area Forum-PC boards._

It seems by 1988 Ken Duda either had walked away or sold his ownership of the software. With MicroTimes, a Bay Area computer magazine [reporting](https://archive.org/details/microtimeissue5100unse/mode/2up?q=%22forum+pc+software%22) in their December 1988 issue. _Chris Rhodes of Requin Systems informs us that the moribund Forum-PC Demonstration BBS and sysop support line is back with us at (415)487-76XX. Chris explains that the board was originally founded by Ken Duda, the author of the Forum-PC software. The board, Chris says, “was taken down a year ago, to the dismay of Forum-PC users. With Ken’s departure for MIT, he's handed over distribution and support of Forum-PC to Requin Systems.”_
